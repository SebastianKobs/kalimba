# Design log

One entry per labelled commit, starting with its short hash: date, label, decision or change, and why.

- `1d1b3e1` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Layout from the hand-drawn card `kalimba.jpg` (rotated 90° clockwise): 21 tongues `D6 B5 G5 E5 C5 A4 F4 D4 B3 G3 [F3] A3 C4 E4 G4 B4 D5 F5 A5 C6 E6`, each labelled like the card with letter, octave dots and cipher digit (1 = C … 7 = B), drawn hanging from a bridge with length ∝ 1/√f:** the card is the instrument's description; a tine's frequency rises with 1/L², so the drawn lengths follow pitch like the real tongues.
- `1d1b3e1` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Input: pointer down plucks a tongue, dragging across tongues strums each once, multi-touch plays chords; computer keys in pitch order, F3–A4 on `Z`…`/` and B4–E6 on `A`…`'`, by `KeyboardEvent.code`; key repeat and Ctrl/Meta/Alt chords ignored:** pitch order on two rows fits 21 keys exactly (10 + 11); physical codes make QWERTZ and QWERTY play the same; browser shortcuts keep working.
- `df74646` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Play-along view: a canvas lane over each tongue with notes falling onto the bridge line, one beat every 64 px with a faint line per beat; the next tongue lights up, a wrong pluck flashes red; modes Wait for me (default; holds at each step, a chord needs all its tongues), Play along (keeps time, hits within ±0.3 beats) and Listen (the kalimba plays it); speed 25–150 %; status shows how the song was fitted and the score:** a learner sees which tongue and when, can go step by step first and then in time, and hears how it should sound.
- `df74646` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Song text format: note names (F#4, Bb3) or the card's cipher numbers (1 = C4, ' up and , down an octave), jianpu durations (_ halves, . adds half, - holds a beat, 0 rests), [ ] chords, title and tempo headers; unreadable text is rejected with line and token; typing in a text field never plucks:** the cipher matches the tongues and the tabs kalimba players use, note names cover sheet music; errors must say what to fix.
- `df74646` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Built-in songs are public domain only (Twinkle Twinkle with bass, Ode to Joy in D, Frère Jacques in F); copyrighted songs such as C418's "Sweden" are pasted in by the player, not transcribed into the repo:** a note-for-note transcription would copy the composition; the general translator makes any pasted tab playable.
- `db18d34` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Pasted kalimba tabs are read as the sites write them: numbers or bare letters (octave 4), octave marks ' ’ ′ * ° º or a dot above (″ ” " for two octaves), , or a dot below; ( ) and [ ] group notes played together; a - after a note holds it a beat longer, also glued to the next note ("2-4" = 2 for two beats, then 4); underlining is lost on copy, so those notes stay one beat:** following the kalimbatabs.net legend; my earlier readings of - as "together" and as "within one beat" both sounded wrong.
- `db18d34` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Unreadable text is skipped and listed under the status, never an error; a line with more unreadable tokens than notes is skipped whole; only text with no notes at all is refused; a lone • is skipped with its reason (above or below can't be told):** a copied page brings headings, lyrics and a "Source:" line; prose words like "A" must not sneak in as notes.
- `db18d34` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Import: the song box is always visible; paste, give a name, optionally a tab tuning (writes a key: line), press Import; the song joins the song list and is selected (this session only); an empty or taken name and text without notes are refused with the reason; a title: line in the text wins over the list name in the status:** the folded box hid importing; the name is how the player finds the song again.
- `db18d34` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Tempo slider in BPM (20–240) replaces the speed percentage; choosing a song or pasting text with a tempo: line sets it, a tab without one keeps the slider; it changes the tempo live:** pasted tabs rarely name a tempo, so the player sets it directly.
- `db18d34` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Transpose list next to Tempo: the ways to fit the song that lose no more notes than needed and fold at most a quarter by an octave, at most six, best fit first; worded as intervals ("down a fifth (7 semitones)"), as is the status:** G major reaches a C kalimba down a fifth (all fits) or up a fourth (closer to the recording, top notes folded); which sounds better is the player's call.
- `ec91b0d` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Per-tongue retuning: a Retune toggle; in retune mode a tap cycles the tongue natural → # → b → natural and plays the new pitch, strumming is off; Reset tuning; place, length, number and keyboard key stay; songs are refitted and a running song stops; the tuning is kept in localStorage (validated on load):** to simulate a kalimba with retuned tines, such as a C kalimba with 4′ raised to F#; one tap per change suits 14 px tongues on a phone better than ♭/♯ buttons.
- `ec91b0d` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **A number in a song names a tongue and sounds as that tongue is tuned (with 4′ raised, 4′ is F#5, 4 stays F4); letters and note names stay pitches; a key: line decides instead, so the two never stack; a written # or b wins:** on a real kalimba a tab's number is a tine; reading 4′ as the pitch F5 left no tongue for it once 4′ was raised.
- `ec91b0d` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Tongues labelled as tabs write them: letter and number with octave marks, primes above (C′ over 1′, D″ over 2″), a dot below for the low tongues (Ḅ over 7̣); primes bigger, heavy, in their own colour; retuned tongues show F# over #4′ in red; falling notes carry name and number with the same marks; # and b everywhere; the tuning line reads "Tuning: Standard C with 4̣, 4 and 4′ raised from F to F#":** the labels match what one types into a song, and a 1′ was easy to mistake for 1.
- `86ace6b` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **While a song runs, the song box turns into a read-only tab view of the text as written, highlighting the part being played (the step to play next when the player plays, the step sounding in Anhören; in a - run only its part, a ( ) group as one) and scrolling with it; the editable box returns on Stopp or at the end; parser, arrangement and session carry each note's place in the text:** the player can check what the app reads against the tab, e.g. whether a 1′ is played an octave too low; a textarea can't highlight parts of its text.
- `86ace6b` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **The interface is German (labels, modes, help, status, errors, intervals such as "eine Quinte tiefer (7 Halbtöne)", the tuning line "Stimmung: Standard-C mit 4′ erhöht von F auf F#", built-in song titles); code, comments and tests stay English; note names stay international (B = German H, Bb = German B), as in tabs from the web; the intro names keyboard rows instead of letters:** the player is German-speaking; pasted tabs use international names and the parser reads them.
- `86ace6b` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Takt list next to Tempo (2/4, 3/4, 4/4 default, 5/4, 6/8, 12/8; a meter: line sets it, other meters join the list, a tab without one keeps the choice); the count-in is one bar; the lane draws a stronger line at each bar; a beat is one note step, so 6/8 counts eighths:** bars and |-lines get a meaning; a rest of a bar is 0 --- in 4/4.
- `14bc1c6` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Tongue key labels as printed on a German (QWERTZ) keyboard: Y for the lowest tongue, - for A4, Ö and Ä for the two highest; keys still play by position; the intro names "Y bis Minus tief, A bis Ä hoch":** the US labels Z / ; ' named the wrong keys on the player's keyboard; navigator.keyboard.getLayoutMap() would read the real layout but Firefox lacks it.
- `950eb46` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **Klang panel below the kalimba: seven German sliders, Obertöne, Helligkeit (300 Hz to 20 kHz by octaves, from 3500 Hz), Anschlag, Körper, Schwebung, Stimmung (±50 Cent), Nachklang (0.25 to 2 ×); a double-click puts one back, Zurücksetzen all; kept in the browser; four move the voice card's values (read from kalimba.yaml), Stimmung and Nachklang shape each pluck; stim-garden's detailed device view was tried and dropped:** a few knobs that each mean something are easier for an end user than every module's; tune and ring length can't be card macros, as the twin's tune and the note's decay time are taken.
- `f84257c` 2026-11-04 DESIGN: **The Klang panel is a drawer built into the kalimba: shut, it lies behind the body with only a brass pull under the belly; pulled, its front, bowed as the belly, slides out with the sliders behind it (0.45 s, none with reduced motion); the page in Waldboden · Rosenrot · Rostkirsche: a dark forest green, rose red for what to press (Start, sliders, targets, notes), pale rose for notes folded an octave, the kalimba in rust cherry with wood grain and a brass bridge; the controls in two panels (Spielen, Liedtext), labels above every field, Start the one filled button:** the user wanted the panel hidden in the kalimba and chose the scheme from mockups built on their own palette (no yellowish accents, a darker green, the cherry wood); the controls were a heap of mixed styles.
